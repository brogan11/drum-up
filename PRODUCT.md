# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three roles share the platform:

- **Restaurant owners / venue managers**: Busy operators who post open music slots and browse musician profiles. They're on desktop or tablet, often multitasking, making quick decisions about who fits their vibe and budget.
- **Musicians and bands**: Independent performers looking for paid gigs nearby. They're on mobile, checking opportunities between sets or on the go. Time-sensitive: open slots fill fast.
- **Fans**: Casual discoverers who follow venues and artists to find live music in their city. Low commitment; they browse more than they book.

## Product Purpose

Drum Up is a two-sided marketplace connecting restaurants seeking live music with musicians looking for performance opportunities. The platform handles discovery, booking, and payment in one place, taking a 5-10% booking fee on confirmed gigs. Pro tiers unlock advanced features for serious venues and working musicians.

Success looks like: a musician in Philadelphia finds a Friday night slot at a nearby restaurant, applies in under a minute, and gets paid through the app without any off-platform negotiation.

## Brand Personality

Energetic, creative, bold. Drum Up should feel like the first chord of a set: immediate, confident, alive. It's a tool for working artists and real venues, not a polished enterprise product or a social network.

Voice: direct, warm, a bit scrappy. Like a well-run local music venue, not a tech company.

## Anti-references

- **Old Facebook**: avoid cluttered notification-driven nav, heavy blue tones, skeuomorphic card layouts, sidebar-everything layouts, social feed patterns that bury the core action.
- **Generic gig economy marketplaces**: avoid the commodity-labor aesthetic where one side clearly browses and the other clearly waits to be picked.

## Design Principles

1. **Location is the product.** Every screen is implicitly about proximity. Distance, neighborhood, and city context should be present without being labored.
2. **Both sides are professionals.** Restaurants and musicians are peers on this platform, not buyer and seller. Neither role should feel subordinate or like a listing in a catalog.
3. **Speed over ceremony.** A musician scanning gigs between rehearsals needs to see what matters in two seconds. A restaurant owner posting a slot shouldn't need a tutorial. Reduce steps, not features.
4. **Energy, not polish.** The brand is warm and alive, not sterile and refined. Prefer density and character over empty whitespace and generic SaaS minimalism.
5. **Trust through transparency.** Payments, fees, and booking status should always be scannable and unambiguous. Trust is the hardest thing to earn in a two-sided marketplace.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as a baseline. Reduce-motion support for any animations. Ensure sufficient contrast against both the light (snow) and dark (graphite) surfaces. Mobile-first: musicians are often on older mid-range Android devices in low-light venues.
